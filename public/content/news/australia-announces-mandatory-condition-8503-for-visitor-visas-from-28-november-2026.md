The Australian Government has officially introduced new immigration regulations that will make the No Further Stay condition (Condition 8503) mandatory in specified circumstances for certain visitor visa applicants. The changes will take effect from **28 November 2026**. 

The amendment, titled Migration Amendment (Preventing Further Stay for Visitor Visas) Regulations 2026, was made on 12 November 2026 and registered on 13 November 2026. It amends the existing Migration Regulations 1994. 

The changes will affect applicants under three visitor visa subclasses: Subclass 600, Subclass 601 and Subclass 651. 

## What Is Changing Under the New Regulations? 

The Australian Government is expanding the circumstances in which Condition 8503 must be imposed on visitor visas. 

Previously, Condition 8503 could be imposed on certain Subclass 600 visa applicants, depending on the visa stream and circumstances. Under the new amendment, it will become mandatory in specified circumstances. 

The amendment also introduces Condition 8503 requirements for Subclass 601 ETA and Subclass 651 eVisitor visas. 

The key changes include: 

* **Subclass 600** - Visitor Visa: The amendment changes the conditions applicable to specified visa applicants, making Condition 8503 mandatory in circumstances where it was previously discretionary. 

* **Subclass 601** - Electronic Travel Authority (ETA): Condition 8503 will be added to the mandatory visa conditions. 

* **Subclass 651** - eVisitor: Condition 8503 will also become a mandatory condition. 

These changes aim to prevent visitors from using temporary visitor visas as a pathway to remain in Australia by applying for other visas after arrival. 

## Understanding Condition 8503 - No Further Stay 

Condition 8503 is commonly known as the No Further Stay condition. 

It restricts visa holders from being granted most other substantive visas while they remain in Australia. 

In practical terms, visitors who receive a visa with Condition 8503 generally cannot switch to another substantive visa from within Australia. 

For example, a visitor who arrives in Australia and later decides to apply for a different visa category may be unable to do so while Condition 8503 applies. 

The condition does not prevent a person from making a protection visa application. Other limited exceptions may also apply under Australian immigration law.

## Will Existing Australian Visitor Visa Holders Be Affected? 

The new amendment includes a transitional provision. 

According to the regulations, the changes apply to visa applications made on or after 28 November 2026. 

This means the new amendments do not automatically change the conditions of visitor visas granted under earlier applications. 

Existing visa holders should continue to follow the conditions specified in their individual visa grant notices. 

## Can Condition 8503 Be Waived? 

Condition 8503 may be waived in limited circumstances. 

Under Australian migration law, a waiver may be considered when compelling and compassionate circumstances arise after the visa is granted, are beyond the visa holder's control and result in a major change in their circumstances. 

However, meeting these requirements does not guarantee approval. Each request is assessed according to the applicable legal provisions. 

Applicants should not assume that they can obtain a waiver simply because they wish to extend their stay or apply for another visa.
