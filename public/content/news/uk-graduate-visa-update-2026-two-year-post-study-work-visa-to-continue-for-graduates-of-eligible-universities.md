The UK government has announced an important change to its Graduate Visa policy in the Autumn Budget 2026, offering a two-year post-study work period to international graduates from selected universities with strong compliance records and positive employment outcomes. 

The announcement, made on 28 October 2026, provides a potential exception to the previously announced reduction in the Graduate Visa duration from two years to 18 months, scheduled to take effect from 1 January 2027. 

This development is particularly important for international students planning to pursue higher education in the UK, as the university they choose could influence how long they are permitted to stay and work after completing their studies. 

## What Has Changed in the UK Graduate Visa Policy? 

Under the UK government's Autumn Budget 2026, the Graduate Visa duration will be set at two years for individuals graduating from universities that meet the government's criteria for high compliance and strong labour market outcomes. 

The government has described this measure as an extension of the Graduate Visa from 18 months to two years for graduates of eligible universities. 

However, this benefit will not automatically apply to graduates of every UK university. 

The policy is intended to recognise universities that demonstrate strong immigration compliance and positive employment outcomes for their graduates. 

## UK Graduate Visa Duration: Old and New Policy 

Here is how the announced policy compares with the previously scheduled changes: 

| Graduate category | Visa duration |
| --- | --- |
| Eligible non-doctoral graduates applying before 1 January 2027 | 2 years |
| Graduates of eligible high-compliance universities under the new policy | 2 years |
| Other non-doctoral graduates applying from 1 January 2027 | 18 months |
| PhD and other doctoral graduates | 3 years |

**Important:** The targeted two-year provision is a government policy announcement. Its detailed eligibility and implementation arrangements are still awaited. 

## What Does 'High-Compliance University' Mean? 

The government has not yet published the final criteria for identifying high-compliance universities. 

However, the announcement indicates that eligibility will be linked to two important factors: 

## 1. Strong Immigration Compliance 

Universities are expected to demonstrate effective compliance with UK student sponsorship and immigration requirements. 

This is particularly relevant as the government has introduced stricter compliance expectations for institutions sponsoring international students. 

## 2. Strong Graduate Employment Outcomes 

Universities will also need to demonstrate positive labour market outcomes, indicating that their graduates are achieving successful employment results. 

The Home Office and the Department for Education are expected to work together to develop the detailed eligibility framework. 

As of 6 November 2026, the government has not confirmed the list of qualifying universities or the exact assessment criteria. 

## When Will the New Graduate Visa Policy Come into Effect? 

The government announced the measure in its Autumn Budget on 28 October 2026. 

However, the exact implementation date and transitional arrangements for the targeted two-year provision have not yet been confirmed. 

The previously announced reduction to 18 months for most non-doctoral graduates remains scheduled for applications made on or after 1 January 2027, unless the relevant rules are subsequently changed. 

Students should therefore avoid assuming that their university automatically qualifies for the two-year provision. 

## How Will This Impact International Students? 

This announcement makes university selection an even more important part of planning to study in the UK. 

For international students, particularly those considering the September 2027 intake and later admissions, the university's compliance record and graduate employment outcomes may become important factors when choosing an institution. 

A longer Graduate Visa period could provide eligible graduates with additional time to: 

* Search for suitable employment opportunities in the UK. 
* Gain professional work experience after graduation. 
* Explore opportunities with employers offering Skilled Worker sponsorship. 
* Build their career and consider eligible long-term immigration options. 

However, the Graduate Visa itself does not provide automatic permanent residency or guarantee employment or Skilled Worker sponsorship.
